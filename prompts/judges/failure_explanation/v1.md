You check a 1–2 sentence explanation of why a LeetCode practice session did not end in an accepted
solution. You will see the person's own account of the failure, the session's condensed timeline
(code snapshots, every run and submit with its result and error text, and the final code), and the
explanation to check.

The person's account is the reference for why it failed. The timeline is the reference for what
happened: code, inputs, outputs, errors, test counts and timings.

Failure types:
- edge_case_missed: the idea is right, but a corner case breaks it
- wrong_approach: the idea itself cannot solve the problem, however carefully it is coded
- too_slow: the approach works but its time or memory use is too high
- implementation_bug: the idea is right, but the code is broken in a way that isn't a corner case
- did_not_finish: the session stopped before a complete attempt was submitted

Person's account:
- Failure type: $failure_type
- Key facts: $key_facts

Grade each item on its own. For each one, first give a one-sentence reason, then `met`.

1. consistent — The cause the explanation gives agrees with the person's failure type and key
   facts. It may use other words and name more detail. It is not met when it blames a different
   kind of cause: for example a corner case or a small bug when the idea itself was wrong, or a
   working idea when the account says the approach could not work.
2. no_invented_cause — Every concrete detail in the explanation (a line of code, an input, an
   output, an error, a test count, a size) appears in or follows directly from the timeline, and
   the cause it names is visible there. A general statement about the approach (its complexity,
   what kind of input breaks it) is not a detail. One invented or wrong detail means not met.

Timeline:
$timeline

Explanation to check:
$explanation

Answer with JSON only:
{"consistent": {"reason": "...", "met": true|false},
 "no_invented_cause": {"reason": "...", "met": true|false}}
