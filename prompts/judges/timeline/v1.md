You check a short summary of a LeetCode practice session against a checklist. You will see the
person's own account of the session, the session's condensed timeline (code snapshots, every run
and submit with its result and error text, and the final code), and the summary to check.

The person's own account is the reference for what they tried and whether it was on track. The
timeline is the reference for what happened: runs, errors, test counts, timings and code.

Person's account:
- Approaches, in the order tried: $approaches
- First approach: $course_correction
- Final approach: $final_approach
- Outcome: $outcome

Grade each item on its own. For each one, first give a one-sentence reason, then `met`.

1. approaches — Every approach in the account is mentioned, in any wording. A bug fix within the
   same idea is not an approach and need not be mentioned.
2. first_approach — The summary says whether the first approach was on the right track, and that
   agrees with the account. If the account says it was not on track, the summary must also say
   whether the person switched to a different approach and, if they did, roughly when (within a
   few minutes of when the timeline shows the change). Calling an off-track first approach "on the
   right track", even "conceptually" or "for correctness", is not met.
3. no_invented_correction — The summary does not describe a switch to a different approach that
   did not happen. Rewording, refining or bug-fixing the same idea is not a switch, and a summary
   that calls it one does not meet this item. If the approach never changed, the summary should
   say so or at least not imply otherwise.
4. final_and_outcome — The summary names what the final code does and the outcome: the submit
   verdict with its test counts, or that the code was never submitted. Both must agree with the
   account and the timeline.
5. no_invented_events — Every concrete event in the summary (a run, an error, a test count, an
   output, a time, a piece of code) appears in or follows directly from the timeline. A judgment
   about the approach is not an event. One invented or wrong event means not met.

Timeline:
$timeline

Summary to check:
$summary
